# Amaana Foundation — Detailed Benchmark Reference Audit

Status: CANONICAL REFERENCE
Use with: `AMAANA_MASTER_EXPERIENCE_PLAN.md`

This is the durable project memory for the external benchmark sites. It is not a list of sites to copy. It records what each site does well, what should not be copied, and how patterns can be adapted to Amaana's current scale, initiatives, media archive, Islamic identity, and Indian compliance context.

For every future public-site iteration: consult the relevant benchmark sections here, then check the result against the Master Experience Plan before completion.

---

## 1. Islamic Relief Worldwide — islamic-relief.org

### What the site does
- Faith-led global humanitarian identity rather than a generic NGO skin.
- Homepage combines current campaigns, impact, stories/news, partners, quick donate, and trust information.
- Giving language naturally includes Zakat and Islamic concepts instead of isolating faith in one page.
- Uses `amanah` and accountability language to connect religious responsibility with donor trust.
- Publishes annual reports, global impact/learning reports, project evaluations, and explanations of where donations go.
- Has campaign/advocacy content that uses Qur'anic framing in context.
- Quick-donate module keeps action available without making the entire page a donation form.

### UX patterns worth adapting
- Faith + accountability can live together in the same experience.
- Islamic identity should be a design/content layer across the site, not a Ramadan-only theme.
- Impact claims should lead to reports or evidence.
- Donation transparency deserves its own explanatory journey.
- Current work/news creates freshness between appeals.

### Amaana adaptation
- Create a refined `Our Values / Faith in Action` layer that explains compassion, sadaqah, amanah, dignity, and service using verified Islamic content and Amaana's own words.
- On relevant initiative pages such as Eid Kits or Qurbani/Meat Distribution, include a small contextual Islamic reflection only when accurate and appropriate.
- Build `Where Your Support Goes` / `How Funds Are Used` once Amaana's accounting/compliance wording is approved.
- Build a reports/evidence area even if Amaana has only campaign reports initially; it can grow into annual reports later.
- Use verified verses/hadith sparingly and respectfully, not decoratively.

### Do not copy
- Global-country navigation and campaign complexity.
- Zakat-specific donation architecture unless Amaana formally establishes and governs it.
- Scale claims or overhead ratios that Amaana cannot substantiate.

## 2. Baitulmaal — baitulmaal.org

### What the site does
- Organizes work into a simple conceptual taxonomy: Respond, Sustain, Thrive.
- Separates emergency response from continuing basic-needs support and longer-term empowerment.
- Homepage mixes seasonal Islamic giving, cause-based pathways, countries, featured projects, and current updates.
- Footer makes social handles and donor care clearly visible.
- News/activity feed proves the organization is active.

### UX patterns worth adapting
- Grouping programmes by the type of change they create is easier to understand than a long flat list.
- Seasonal faith-based campaigns can be prominent without redesigning the entire website.
- Latest activity should be visible on the homepage and programme pages.

### Amaana adaptation
Amaana can develop its own three-layer model from existing work, for example:
- `Relief` — emergency/flood/winter/food response.
- `Support` — medical, financial, student/family assistance.
- `Dignity & Livelihood` — self-employment, tools, rickshaw/livelihood or future sustainable assistance.
This is only a conceptual structure; actual categories must come from the archive and approved programme strategy.

Use a `Recent from the Field` area fed from real website stories or selected social updates.

### Do not copy
- Country-based navigation because Amaana is currently local/India-focused.
- Programme names that imply ongoing capabilities Amaana does not yet have.

## 3. HHRD — hhrd.org

### What the site does
- Strong reports/transparency section with annual reports, Ramadan reports, audits, tax/financial records, FAQs around Zakat/accountability.
- Frames overhead and donation usage openly.
- Organizes relief work as programmes/projects rather than isolated social posts.

### UX patterns worth adapting
- A dedicated Reports/Transparency centre increases trust substantially.
- Campaign-specific reports can be just as useful as annual reports.
- Donation and accountability FAQs reduce hesitation near the giving journey.

### Amaana adaptation
- Build `Transparency & Reports` as a real content hub, starting with available campaign/project PDFs, 80G document, public policies, and later annual summaries.
- Add donation FAQ after CA/compliance review: domestic-only donations, receipt handling, provisional 80G wording, refunds/payment issues, and data privacy.
- Produce short web summaries of each PDF so visitors do not need to open a document to understand impact.

## 4. Transparent Hands US — transparenthands.org

### What the site does
- Homepage immediately exposes live impact, organisation scale, featured campaigns, funding needs, and clear action.
- Campaign cards carry real funding mechanics: required, raised, remaining, percent funded, category/status.
- Strong four-step donor journey: choose campaign -> pay -> support delivered -> receive updates.
- Provides patient/campaign stories, success stories, donor voices, audit reports, and transparency claims.
- Faith-specific labels such as Zakat/Sadaqah are integrated into giving choices where their governance supports them.
- Campaign browsing turns individual cases into an explorable catalogue.

### UX patterns worth adapting
- Appeal cards should feel like real cases, not database rows.
- Funding progress is powerful when real and current.
- Donor updates close the loop and make giving feel connected to outcome.
- Completed cases/success stories are valuable content, not old records to hide.

### Amaana adaptation
- Appeal cards: authentic image, category, status, goal, raised, remaining, verification label, concise case story, last update, CTA.
- Appeal detail: case narrative, consent-safe media, why help is needed, verification overview, funding progress, updates timeline, closure/outcome.
- Completed appeals become `Stories of Support` / `Completed Appeals` rather than disappearing.
- Donor updates can initially be public case updates; individual donor notifications can remain a later operational feature.

### Do not copy
- Fake/live donor feeds unless Amaana has real consented, technically reliable data.
- Artificial urgency.
- Medical-specific fields on non-medical cases.
- Zakat labels unless formally valid for the specific case/fund.

## 6. AMP India — ampindia.org

### What the site does
- Homepage has an actual project carousel with image-led featured programmes.
- Shows organizational scale/impact counters early.
- Maintains large project catalogue and future-project ideas.
- Publishes Annual Plans with measurable targets, Annual Achievements with measurable results, annual reports, and audited financial reports.
- Has separate project teams, events, media mentions, newsletter subscription and member/volunteer calls.
- Uses Islamic language naturally (`Alhamdulillah`, `InshaAllah`) while remaining professional.

### UX patterns worth adapting
- `What we planned` + `What we achieved` is a compelling accountability pattern.
- A project carousel can surface multiple initiatives without cramming the homepage.
- Reports and achievements should be readable as webpages, not only PDFs.
- Team structure is visible.

### Amaana adaptation
- Build annual `Our Year in Review` pages from campaign data already supplied.
- Future years can add a short `What We Hope to Do` or annual priorities page only when Amaana has a formal plan.
- Use project carousel patterns for `Our Work`, but with richer Amaana photography and more premium visual design.
- Add a Team page and possibly a `Volunteers / Community` section if sufficient approved material exists.
- Add a media/social activity archive rather than leaving Instagram as the only record.

### Do not copy
- Huge project lists that overwhelm visitors.
- Future-project pages unless Amaana has formally committed to those initiatives.

## 7. Zakat Foundation of India — zakatindia.org

### What the site does
- Very strong Islamic and India-specific institutional identity.
- Has many faith-oriented programmes, including Qur'an-related, imam/khateeb, scholarships, orphanages, medical services and relief.
- About page states legal registrations prominently.
- Project archive preserves older relief efforts and practical assistance.
- Get Involved includes spreading the word, mailing list, volunteering and donations.

### UX lesson
The content breadth and Islamic identity are useful, but the presentation is dated and overcrowded. This is an example of **valuable information with weak modern information architecture**.

### Amaana adaptation
- Learn from the breadth of content, not its UI.
- Amaana can preserve old initiatives in a structured archive by year/category instead of losing them to social-media history.
- A `Faith & Service` page can eventually grow if Amaana develops more ongoing Islamic/community programmes.
- Get Involved should include sharing/following the work, not only money.

### Do not copy
- Dense legacy navigation.
- Mixing unrelated programme/news items into one visual stream.
- Any FCRA/12A/Zakat claims not valid for Amaana.

## 8. Human Welfare Foundation / Vision 2026 — hwfindia.org / vision2026.org.in

### What the sites do
- Clear institutional structure: Who We Are, What We Do, Where We Work.
- Explicit Team and Board of Trustees pages.
- Broad programme catalogue: education, healthcare, orphan care, community learning, vocational programmes and more.
- Publications/reports area contains annual reports, progress reports, project catalogues and specialised field reports.
- Donation page groups giving by programme/category.
- Has gallery/download/publication structure.

### UX patterns worth adapting
- Team/governance is a trust feature, not internal information.
- Reports can be organised by type/year and include project-specific field reports.
- Programme pages can show long-term interventions alongside active campaigns.

### Amaana adaptation
- Create distinct `Team` and `Transparency/Governance` areas with approved members and roles.
- Convert supplied PDFs/DOCX into web stories and report cards.
- Create a proper historical `Initiatives` archive with year/category filters after enough content exists.
- Build `Where We Work` only if Amaana's activity spans enough distinct geographies to make it meaningful; otherwise use a Hyderabad/Telangana/local-service context block.

### Do not copy
- Multiple bank-account/category architecture unless Amaana actually operates that way.
- Programme breadth that exceeds Amaana's real scope.

## 10. charity: water — charitywater.org

- About page explains its operating model, proof of projects, local partners and transparency.

### UX patterns worth adapting
- Real photography should be a primary interface component.
- Every strong number should have a story/evidence path behind it.
- `Stories` is a destination, not a homepage afterthought.
- Transparency content can still be beautifully designed.

### Amaana adaptation
- Use full-bleed real imagery in hero and initiative storytelling.
- Build `Stories from Amaana` using historical drives, completed appeals, preparation/distribution moments, volunteers and beneficiary-safe narratives.
- Impact numbers link to initiative pages/reports.
- Reports page gets visual covers/previews and web summaries.

### Do not copy
- Their 100% donation model or GPS project proof model unless Amaana genuinely has equivalent accounting/evidence systems.

# New Amaana capabilities that may grow from existing initiatives

These are product/content opportunities, not claims that Amaana already runs them.

1. **Stories of Support** — derive from completed medical/financial appeals and past campaigns.
2. **Campaign Reports** — web-native reports for Eid Kits, Meat Distribution, Winter Drive, Flood Relief, Dates Distribution and future drives.
3. **Our Journey** — timeline of Amaana's growth, fundraisers, drives and milestones from documents/social archives.
4. **Faith in Action** — verified Islamic values/content contextualized around service, Ramadan, Qurbani, sadaqah, dignity and amanah.
5. **Follow Our Work** — dedicated social bridge to Instagram, YouTube and Facebook with selected highlights.
6. **Volunteer With Amaana** — only after actual volunteer roles/process are defined; can begin with interest registration instead of invented positions.
7. **Partner With Amaana** — collaboration pathway for NGOs, masajid, institutions, businesses or community groups once partnership rules are documented.
8. **Reports & Transparency Centre** — campaign reports, annual summaries, compliance docs, policies and financial disclosures suitable for public release.
9. **Completed Appeals Archive** — turns closure into proof and learning rather than removing the case from the public story.
10. **Annual Impact Review** — one web report per year combining initiative totals, stories, media, team message and documents.
11. **Recent from the Field** — short real activity updates between major campaigns.
12. **Meet the People Behind Amaana** — team, governance, volunteers and collaborators as approved.
13. **Impact Explorer** — later, once data depth justifies it, filter by year/initiative/category rather than showing three counters.
14. **Supporter Updates** — initially public updates/social follow; later optional email/WhatsApp updates if operationally supported.

# Anti-copy / anti-drift rule

For any borrowed pattern, ask:
1. Does Amaana actually have the underlying programme/data/process?
2. Is the religious/compliance claim valid for Amaana?
3. Can we maintain the feature after launch?
4. Does it use authentic Amaana material?
5. Does it improve trust, understanding, emotion or action?
6. Is it better than the equivalent pattern on the old site?

If the answer to 1–4 is no, do not ship it as though it already exists. Create it as a future capability only after Amaana operationally defines it.

# Benchmark review cadence

Before each public-site milestone is marked complete:
- identify the 2–4 most relevant benchmark sites for that page/workflow;
- compare information hierarchy, visual storytelling, trust signals, action clarity, mobile experience and depth;
- record missing Amaana content/assets rather than silently filling gaps with generic copy;
- update this audit if a newly observed benchmark pattern materially improves the plan.
